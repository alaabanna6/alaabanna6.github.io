At a glance

The problem

People building new skills learn across Coursera, Udemy, LinkedIn Learning and YouTube. Each one tracks progress its own way, so nobody can see their overall growth or how close they are to a career goal.

My role

A solo project that started from a problem I had as a learner. I ran the research, defined the structure and flows, designed every screen and tested the prototype.

The result

A tested prototype that scored 88.33 on UMUX Lite, a Grade A result, with five participants. Two issues found in testing shaped the final design.

01 · The challenge

Four platforms, four ways of measuring progress

Picture Sarah, a marketing coordinator moving into UX design. She learns on three platforms in short sessions on her phone, and she cannot tell how far she is from her first UX role.

Comparing the major platforms showed the problem was structural. No shared standard exists for what progress means.

Coursera

Certificates and course completion.

Udemy

Percentage of video watched.

LinkedIn Learning

Hours spent learning.

YouTube

No tracking at all.

The answer

StageUp turns all learning activity into skill tags, weighted by depth of engagement. A finished course counts for more than part of a video, and a course with a hands on project counts for more than passive watching. Every other part of the product depends on this one decision.

02 · Key decisions

Four decisions, each tied to evidence

01

The career goal anchors every screen

The dashboard always shows how far the user is from the goal and what is still missing. Finishing a course stops being the finish line. The user reads 67% of the way to UX Designer.

Based on

Platform research. Recommendations tied to a real career goal keep learners coming back longer than generic course suggestions.

02

Four numbers answer how am I doing

The home screen shows skill growth, weekly activity, current streak and goal progress with no scrolling. Each card answers one question a learner has the moment the app opens.

Based on

How people use it. Learners check progress in short breaks and commutes, so the first screen has to be read at a glance.

03

Growth is a line that climbs

Skill score is drawn as a chart over recent weeks, so the user sees momentum building. A line that rises is harder to ignore than a single static number.

Based on

Platform research. Visual progress keeps learners motivated far better than text summaries.

04

Sign up and log in became separate paths

New users choose Get started and returning users choose Log in, each with its own flow. One unclear screen had been the only thing between a new user and the product.

Based on

Usability testing. A participant hesitated at the combined screen and could not tell whether the app saw them as new or returning.

03 · Testing

Tested with five people, then redesigned

Five participants used the mid fidelity prototype. Each one connected a learning source, set a career goal and found their way around the dashboard, then completed a UMUX Lite questionnaire.

Sign up was confusing

One participant hesitated after entering an email. The difference between a new and a returning user was not clear.

What changed

Two separate screens, Get started and Log in, each with a dedicated flow.

Greyscale felt flat

Two participants found the grey palette hard to connect with. One called the app visually plain.

What changed

A full colour system with green for growth, colour coded icons, real course imagery and stronger type hierarchy.

A second round on the high fidelity prototype confirmed both fixes. Participants knew at once which path was theirs, and described the interface as clean, motivating and easy to use.

One more fix, for accessibility

A contrast check on the finished colour system showed the original green failed WCAG AA. I replaced it with a darker green across every button, active state and progress indicator.

04 · The product

From first launch to a personal dashboard

The flow came first. A new user connects their learning platforms, sets a career goal and lands on a dashboard built around that goal.

Welcome

Four benefits, then two clear paths for new and returning users.

Connect sources

Step one of three. Pick the platforms to sync, or skip for now.

Set a career goal

Choose a goal and a timeline. Everything after this points at it.

Day one home

A new user sees the goal and where to start, not empty charts.

Home

Four numbers and one chart answer how am I doing.

Courses

Courses from every platform in one list, with time remaining.

Skills

Strong and developing skills, scored from all learning activity.

Goal

Progress toward the role, broken into milestones.

05 · Takeaway

Nobody wants synced platforms. They want the career.

Reframing the problem around career goals, and away from course completion, shaped every decision that followed. The hardest part of StageUp was structural. It meant building one meaningful system from learning data that was never designed to work together.
